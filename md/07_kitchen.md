# Kitchen

Tomás was cutting onions the way his mother had taught him, which was the wrong way, and Emi was watching him do it while pretending to read a book.

"You're holding the knife like a hammer," she said, without looking up.

"I know how to cut an onion."

"I'm just saying."

He did not change his grip. The onion was falling into uneven pieces on the board, some the size of dice and some the size of postage stamps. This did not matter for the soup, which would cook everything into sameness anyway, but it mattered to Emi, who cooked like a person defusing a bomb.

"There's a video," she said. "I could send it to you."

"Please don't send me a video."

"It's two minutes."

"It's about onions."

She turned a page she had not read. The kitchen smelled of garlic from something earlier, something neither of them had made together, and outside the window the light was the color of an old photograph. It was Tuesday. It had been a long day. Neither of them had said anything about it yet.

"I saw your brother," she said.

He kept cutting. "Where."

"At the bank. He was in line. I didn't say hi."

"Why not."

"He looked like he didn't want to be said hi to."

Tomás nodded. This was a thing she could tell about people, and he had learned to trust it over the years, even when he disagreed. He finished the onion and pushed it into the pot with the flat of the knife, which was also wrong, and which she also let pass.

"He didn't call me," he said.

"No."

"It's been three weeks."

"I know."

She put the book down and came over to the stove and stood next to him, not quite touching, just inside the warm radius of the burner. After a moment she put her hand on his back, briefly, the way you'd touch a railing to confirm it was still there, and then she took the hand away and went to the sink to rinse a carrot neither of them had asked for.

"I'll call him," Tomás said.

"Okay."

"After dinner."

"Okay."

The onions began to hiss. Outside, somewhere, a dog barked twice and stopped.
